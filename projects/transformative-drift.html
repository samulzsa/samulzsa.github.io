Transformative Drift

2023

The sculptures originate from an iterative digital process: text-to-image generation fed back through image-to-3D conversion, each cycle accumulating distortion until the forms drift beyond recognition. The endpoint was determined by material. When geometry exceeded what clay printing could structurally support, the process stopped.

Clay carries associations of craft, organicism, the hand. But the production here involved none of that. The forms were generated algorithmically, the printing mechanical. What reads as artisanal warmth is entirely mediated.

Timur Si-Qin’s position on materiality informed the work: even seemingly immaterial things, a poem, an algorithm, a 3D file, possess materiality because they affect their environment. By this logic, the digital forms already held material presence before any physical translation occurred.

And yet they were printed. The question the work poses to itself: if the digital is already material, why make it physical? The answer isn’t resolution. It’s demonstration. The clay objects are received differently than the files they came from. They enter rooms, occupy space, get called sculpture. The same forms, the same decisions, the same authorship. But now legible to systems that struggle to value what remains on screen.

The printing doesn’t add meaning. It makes existing meaning visible to those who couldn’t see it before.
