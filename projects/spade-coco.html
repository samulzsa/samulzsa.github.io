Spade CoCo

2020

The work uses SPADE-COCO, an early semantic image synthesis model that interpreted color-coded input as object classes. Source material consisted of children’s drawings, fed through the system, their naive color choices were misread as landscape elements, furniture, organic forms. The outputs carried an accidental dreamlike quality, emerging from the gap between intended mark and machine interpretation.

These images were translated into physical prints through a deliberately contradictory process. Halftone rasters, a technique developed for industrial mass reproduction, addressed low resolution of AI models of the time, while introducing a visual language associated with print media. The plates were laser-engraved into wood, then hand-pressed as CMYK separations.

The method is legible as craft while containing none of its traditional gestures. The hand never touched the plate. The wood grain reads as warmth and authenticity, but the cutting was mechanical. What appears as artisanal process is entirely mediated.

The digital images existed before any of this. The creative act, selecting the drawings, running the model, recognizing something worth keeping, happened on screen. The physical translation added labor and material but no additional artistic decision.

Yet the prints were received differently. The question embedded in the work is not rhetorical: where does value actually enter?
