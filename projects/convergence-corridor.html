Convergence Corridor

Licht.Wege Kassel2024

Forty self-built LED tubes installed along a forest path for the light art festival Licht.Wege. The tubes shifted through colors, pulsing along the corridor. Motion sensors on both sides triggered light sequences responding to visitors passing through.

The design intended for participatory movement: visitors walking with the light pulses, becoming part of the work. Reception differed from expectation. Visitors stopped mid-path, sometimes blocking it entirely, observing the light play in the trees above. The interactive element mattered less than the atmospheric experience of the space itself.

This was the first opportunity to observe audiences anonymously, standing among visitors without being identified as the artist. Reactions varied widely. Families with children engaged immediately. Some visitors took photographs, others complained about brightness levels. Some passed through without pausing.

The experience clarified something about shared experience as artistic value. Designing for a specific interaction does not guarantee that interaction. Audiences bring their own attention, their own priorities. The work provides conditions, not outcomes. Connection happens on terms the artist does not fully control, and not everyone will connect at all. Working with this rather than against it became part of subsequent practice.

Project management and concept were my responsibility. The collective handled electronics, coding, and installation.
