Visual Instruments

Master Thesis2025

My master thesis investigates how sustained practice with digital Visual Instruments influences the development of embodied knowledge and auratic presence in live performance.

Visual Instruments are complete performance systems combining generative patches, reusable tools, and user interfaces for real-time visual control, developed in TouchDesigner for live audiovisual contexts.

The research engages Walter Benjamin’s concept of aura: the unique presence of an artwork in time and space, which he argued mechanical reproduction eliminates. Live visual performance presents a paradox, utilizing technologies of infinite reproducibility while creating unrepeatable, site-specific experiences. Can sustained practice with digital instruments restore auratic qualities Benjamin considered lost?

The methodology shifted through necessity. Initial work focused on developing a modular toolbox for rapid instrument creation, technically successful, but unable to answer questions about embodied knowledge. This demanded transformation toward evocative autoethnography: ethnographic research with practitioners, autobiographical documentation across performance contexts, and reflection within broader cultural discourse.

Findings challenged assumptions. Flow states proved democratic, others achieved flow within minutes on unfamiliar interfaces. But sustained practice enables sophisticated expression within flow and develops the capacity to invite others into shared aesthetic experiences. Auratic qualities emerged not through technical sophistication but through visible embodied engagement: temporal uniqueness through real-time decision-making, spatial specificity through contextual reception, collective ritual through shared witness.

The central contribution: tension between technological development and artistic depth isn’t resolved through better systems. Depth emerges through sustained engagement, through vulnerability rather than optimization.
