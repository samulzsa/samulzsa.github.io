Biennale Schwabach2023

An interactive installation exploring gold as connective material on printed circuit boards. PCBs are ubiquitous in daily life, present in smartphones, coffee machines, ticket terminals. The installation in Franzosenkirche Schwabach made this usually invisible layer of technical infrastructure tangible. Visitors touched five displayed circuit boards, directing a musical atmosphere that controlled the church organ. Unpredictable sounds and vibrations emerged from physical contact with the material.

My contribution focused on creating the interwoven organic lines on the PCBs using reaction diffusion simulation. The lines needed to serve a specific function: when a finger glides across the board, different circuits trigger different sounds. This required bringing an organic, self-organizing system into a form that would actually work as interface.

The process involved many iterations, balancing control and emergence. Reaction diffusion follows its own logic. The challenge was guiding it toward functional outcomes without forcing it into something it resists. Never full control, but enough to serve the purpose.

This connected to recurring concerns in my practice: mechanical production mediating artistic gesture, using systems in unintended ways, finding the point where tool and intention meet without either fully dominating. Project concept was developed collectively. My responsibility was the generative line system and visual design of the PCBs.
