about

My practice developed through a persistent question: where does value enter when art moves between digital and physical? Early works examined this directly. Through procedural systems and live performance, an answer emerged: value lives in the moment of shared experience.

This understanding organizes my work into three directions.

As an artist, I examine the intersection of digital and physical production, questioning where value enters when work moves between mediums. Working primarily with procedural systems, I explore how embodied engagement creates presence in digital art. The work often uses tools against their intended purpose, finding meaning in the gaps between systems.

As an educator, I develop courses that approach technical tools as means for artistic expression. The focus is process over outcome, helping students find their own voice through sustained engagement rather than software proficiency.

As part of the collective Latenter Raum, I contribute to public art installations exploring the relationship between human and machine. The collective creates site-specific, interactive works that make technological systems tangible for broad audiences.
